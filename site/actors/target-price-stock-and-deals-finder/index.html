Target Price, Stock & Deals Finder

Pull live product data straight from Target.com — keyword search, a whole department, exact item numbers, a pasted product link, or the current deals rail. Every record carries pricing, sale/clearance flags, online availability, ratings, UPC/DPCI identifiers, category breadcrumbs and product specifications.

What this actor does

  • Five modes: search by keyword, byCategory to browse a department, byTcin to fetch exact items, byUrl to resolve a pasted Target link, and deals for Target's current deals
  • Rich pricing: current price, regular price, currency, savings, discount percent and price type (reg, sale, clearance; marked-down product pages without a Target label are reported as sale)
  • Availability: online stock status plus a shipping flag when Target marks the item as shippable; listings with no stock signal are reported as UNKNOWN
  • Product identifiers: TCIN, UPC, DPCI, category ID, category name and breadcrumbs
  • Review signal: average rating, rating count and written-review count
  • Product content: description, specifications, feature bullets, primary image and direct product URL
  • Server-side sort: relevance, featured, price low/high, highest rated, best sellers and newest
  • Filters: price range, minimum rating, on-sale-only and in-stock-only, applied before records count toward maxItems
  • Pagination: follows Target's result pages up to 50 pages per query (about 1,200 products), deduplicating by TCIN
  • Empty fields are omitted from each record, so a product without a brand or without review data simply won't have those keys

Output per product

  • tcin — Target item number, the unique product identifier
  • title — product name
  • brand — brand name
  • description — product description text (with full-detail enrichment)
  • price — current selling price (USD)
  • originalPrice — regular/list price (USD)
  • priceCurrency — currency code, normally USD
  • priceType — reg, sale, clearance or another Target price label; sale is inferred when a product page is marked down but carries no label
  • isOnSale — true when the item is on sale or clearance
  • saleSavings — dollar amount saved versus the regular price
  • discountPercent — percent saved versus the regular price
  • rating — average customer rating (0–5)
  • ratingCount — total number of customer ratings
  • reviewCount — number of written reviews
  • dpci — Target department/class/item number (full-detail enrichment)
  • upc — product barcode (full-detail enrichment)
  • categoryId / categoryName — Target category identifiers
  • breadcrumbs[] — category path with names, IDs and URLs
  • specifications — product specification key/value pairs (full-detail enrichment)
  • featureBullets[] — specification and feature bullets
  • primaryImageUrl — main product image URL
  • availability — IN_STOCK, LIMITED_STOCK, OUT_OF_STOCK, or UNKNOWN (search, department and deals listings do not expose a stock signal; includeDetails resolves it from the product page)
  • fulfillment — { shipping, pickup, sameDay } booleans when Target exposes them
  • position — 1-based result position in this run
  • productUrl / sourceUrl — direct link to the product's Target.com page
  • recordType — always "product"
  • siteName — always "Target"
  • scrapedAt — ISO 8601 timestamp of when the record was collected

Use cases

  • Price monitoring — track price, originalPrice, priceType and saleSavings for a watchlist of TCINs over time
  • Assortment research — browse a department with byCategory to see what's listed right now (add includeDetails for per-item stock status)
  • Deal discovery — run the deals mode on a schedule to catch newly featured promotions
  • Review benchmarking — compare rating, ratingCount and reviewCount across competing products
  • Catalog enrichment — resolve saved Target URLs into structured records with UPC, DPCI, specifications and category breadcrumbs
  • Identifier matching — join Target products to other catalogs by TCIN, UPC or DPCI

FAQ

Where does this data come from? Target's own publicly crawlable product pages — the server-rendered version of Target.com that search engines index. Search, department and deals pages expose titles, prices, ratings and identifiers; product pages add stock status, UPC/DPCI, breadcrumbs and specifications. The actor needs no Target account, cookie or API key.

What is a TCIN? TCIN stands for Target Corporation Item Number — Target's internal product identifier, visible in product URLs after /A-.

Do I need a Target account or a proxy? No. All modes read publicly listed product data without a login, cookie or API key. The actor picks its network route automatically.

What's the difference between the five modes? search and byCategory return lists of products — one from a keyword query, the other from a department keyword. byTcin and byUrl return exact products, addressed by item number or a pasted link. deals returns Target's current deals.

Why is a field like brand or rating sometimes missing? Target doesn't populate every field for every listing — some products have no brand attribution, and newly listed items may not yet have reviews. Missing fields are omitted rather than returned empty.

What does includeDetails do? It fetches each product page to add the description, UPC/DPCI, category breadcrumbs, specifications and the online stock status Target publishes. It is on by default; turn it off for the fastest, lightest run. The daily test uses the light path.

Why is availability sometimes UNKNOWN? Target's search, department and deals listings (the pages this actor reads without extra requests) do not publish a per-item stock status. Rather than guessing, the actor marks those records UNKNOWN; enable includeDetails or look up the item with byTcin/byUrl to get the status from its product page. The inStockOnly filter still removes every item Target explicitly reports as out of stock.

Can I combine the price, rating, and stock filters? Yes. minPrice, maxPrice, minRating, onSaleOnly and inStockOnly all apply together, and only products passing every filter count toward maxItems.

How many products can one run return? Up to 1,000, set by maxItems, and at most 50 result pages per query (maxPages). Target caps a search at about 1,200 products; for broader coverage, run again with a different query, categoryId or sortBy.

Why do some runs return fewer products than maxItems? Target may have fewer matches, the filters may reject some, or the source may reach its 50-page ceiling. The actor never fabricates rows to fill the quota.

Does the actor return store-level pickup stock? No. Target's per-store stock API is protected by an anti-bot challenge and is not reachable from this actor's network. availability reflects Target's public online stock status (and is UNKNOWN when the source page carries no signal); store-level pickup quantities are not included.

How reliable is the data route? The actor reads Target's public server-rendered pages and tries the cheapest network route first, escalating automatically if a response lacks the expected product data. This path is public and cookieless, but it is a search-engine view of the catalogue: it can lag the interactive site and Target may change its markup. The actor never invents records to fill a quota — a run reports fewer items rather than fabricating them.

Are product variations included? Target's public product page exposes the selected variation's price and identifiers but not the full variation list. The actor returns the item you asked for; it does not enumerate every color or size.

Is this affiliated with Target? No. This is an independent, third-party actor and is not operated by or affiliated with Target Corporation.
