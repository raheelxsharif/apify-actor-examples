    GitLab Careers Job Listings & Company Profile Extractor is an Apify Actor published by Dataspryva. It accepts 16 input fields, none of them required at the top level. Each result can carry up to 11 declared fields, including recordType, title, companyName, location, workplaceType and division. It costs $0.99 per 1,000 results on Apify's Free plan and $0.49 on the Business plan.

    What it does

    Pull GitLab's live hiring data straight from its own public Greenhouse boards: every open role, every future-opportunity expression-of-interest form, and a rich snapshot of GitLab's careers page. No browser, no login, no API key.

    Data sources

    GitLab publishes its openings through Greenhouse's public job board API and its careers site on about.gitlab.com. The actor reads those public endpoints directly. It does not use cookies, logins, or paid proxies, and it is not affiliated with or endorsed by GitLab.

    The live board API is authoritative for openings; GitLab's rendered all-jobs page can lag behind it and can show stale counts. The actor always reports what the live board currently returns.

    Modes

    Mode What it does
    searchJobs Keyword search across title, company, location, requisition ID, department, and division
    allJobs Every open GitLab role, with optional filters
    byDepartment Roles in a department or anywhere below it in the department tree
    byJobIds Resolve exact Greenhouse job IDs (both GitLab boards are checked)
    byJobUrls Resolve exact Greenhouse job URLs for gitlab and gitlabcrm
    futureOpportunities GitLab's future-role expression-of-interest forms
    companyProfile One company record built from the GitLab careers page and live board totals

    Use cases

    • Recruiting research — track which roles GitLab has open in a department, division, office, or country
    • Compensation research — collect published US and country salary ranges with their original labels
    • Talent-market monitoring — watch firstPublishedAt and updatedAt to see hiring velocity
    • Sales intelligence — filter by quotaCoverageType to see GitLab's sales-role mix
    • Job-board aggregation — feed live GitLab postings into a larger jobs dataset
    • Employer-brand research — pull GitLab's careers tagline, stats, social links, and handbook links in one call
    • Future-role tracking — monitor GitLab's expression-of-interest forms alongside open roles

    What you get

    • Seven modes: keyword search, all open jobs, jobs in a department, exact job IDs, exact job URLs, future opportunities, and company profile
    • Salary data: GitLab's structured US salary ranges, plus non-US ranges published in job descriptions (for example Poland), with min, max, currency, and the original range title
    • Department intelligence: leaf department, full department path, and top-level division for every role, so a filter on Engineering also returns roles in Data Engineering, AI Engineering, and the rest of the tree
    • Filters: department, division, office, quota-coverage type, location text, remote-only, publish date, and update date
    • Full descriptions on request: HTML, plain text, section headings, outbound links, and compliance types
    • Future opportunities: GitLab's expression-of-interest forms live on a separate Greenhouse board; include them with a toggle or a dedicated mode
    • Company profile: careers tagline, company stats, logo, social links, useful GitLab careers/handbook URLs, live opening counts, and division counts

    Output records

    Each dataset item has recordType: job, futureOpportunity, company, or error.

    Jobs and future opportunities

    • jobId — Greenhouse job ID
    • url — direct Greenhouse posting URL
    • title — job title
    • companyName — employer name from the board
    • board — gitlab for open roles, gitlabcrm for future opportunities
    • location — location string from the posting
    • workplaceType — remote or on-site, derived from the location string
    • isRemote — boolean remote flag
    • language — posting language code
    • requisitionId — GitLab's requisition number
    • internalJobId — GitLab's internal job ID, when present
    • firstPublishedAt, updatedAt — ISO timestamps
    • applicationDeadline — ISO timestamp, when GitLab publishes one
    • metadata — additional board metadata, when present
    • quotaCoverageType — sales quota-coverage classification, when present
    • departmentNames — leaf department names
    • departmentPaths — full paths, for example Data Engineering > Engineering
    • division / divisions — top-level division(s)
    • officeNames — attached GitLab offices or regions
    • salaryRanges — every known range with title, min, max, currency, and source (greenhouse or description)
    • salaryMin, salaryMax, salaryCurrency — flattened first range
    • salaryRangeTitle, salaryRangeBlurb — first range's label and GitLab's explanatory text
    • descriptionHtml, descriptionText — full description, when fetchFullDescription is enabled
    • sectionHeadings — headings found in the description
    • descriptionLinks — outbound links found in the description
    • complianceTypes — data-compliance classifications
    • aiDisclaimer, aiOptOutRequestUrl, includeAiDisclaimer — when GitLab publishes them
    • scrapedAt — when the record was fetched

    Salary ranges are conditional: GitLab attaches structured US ranges to some postings and publishes country-specific ranges in some descriptions. When neither exists, salary fields are omitted rather than guessed.

    • careersUrl, allJobsUrl, benefitsUrl, valuesUrl, handbookUrl

    • logoUrl — GitLab careers logo

    Questions

    Is this affiliated with GitLab?

    No. This is an independent, third-party actor that reads GitLab's public job boards and careers page. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by GitLab.

    Do I need a login, cookie, or API key?

    No. GitLab's Greenhouse boards and careers page are public, and the actor reads them without authentication or proxy setup.

    What is the difference between searchJobs and allJobs?

    searchJobs requires a keyword. allJobs returns open roles without a keyword, so you can apply only the other filters.

    Why do some jobs have no salary fields?

    GitLab publishes structured US salary ranges for many US roles, and some non-US roles carry country-specific ranges in their descriptions. When GitLab publishes no range for a role, the actor omits salary fields instead of guessing.

    Why are future opportunities a separate mode?

    GitLab's expression-of-interest forms live on a second Greenhouse board (gitlabcrm). Use futureOpportunities to list only those forms, or set includeFutureOpportunities to add them to another list mode.

    How does department filtering work across parents?

    GitLab postings carry leaf departments. The actor resolves the full department tree, so filtering on Engineering also matches Data Engineering, AI Engineering, and every other engineering team.

    Is there pagination?

    Greenhouse returns the whole board in one response, so there is no page cursor. maxItems caps the dataset, and the source is read in a single request.

    Why do the careers page and the dataset sometimes show different counts?

    The rendered careers page can lag behind the live board. The actor reports the live board totals and the live posting set.

    What happens with an invalid job ID or URL?

    The actor emits a typed error record with errorType: "not_found" and continues with the other identifiers. Invalid input, such as a missing required field, produces an invalid_input error record.

    Can I get closed jobs or historical openings?

    No. GitLab's public board only exposes currently published postings and future-opportunity forms. Keeping a history would require repeated runs and your own storage.

    What does companyProfile return?

    One record with the GitLab careers-page tagline, summary, section headings, stats, logo, social links, useful GitLab URLs, live open-role counts, and division counts.

    Limits and notes

    Limitations

    • GitLab does not publish a salary range for every role; salary fields are conditional.
    • applicationDeadline and the AI-disclaimer fields exist in Greenhouse's schema but GitLab rarely or never populates them, so they are usually absent.
    • Closed or expired postings are not available from the live board.
    • The rendered all-jobs page can show stale counts; the actor uses the live board as the source of truth.
