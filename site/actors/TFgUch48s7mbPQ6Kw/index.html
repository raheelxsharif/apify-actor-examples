Zillow Distressed Property Finder by City or ZIP

Zillow keeps its foreclosure, pre-foreclosure, pre-market REO and auction inventory inside its regular map search. This actor applies those distress filters server-side for you and returns structured records for any US city, ZIP code, neighborhood or county, with optional per-listing detail enrichment: listing agent, phone, brokerage, photos, description, year built, HOA and per-row distress flags.

What this actor does

  • Searches one or more locations: city ("Austin, TX"), ZIP ("78701"), neighborhood ("Hyde Park, Austin, TX") or county ("Travis County, TX").
  • Also accepts Zillow search URLs you already have; the distress filter is layered on top of the URL's own filters.
  • Restricts results to the distress categories you pick: foreclosures (including bank-owned), pre-foreclosures, pre-market foreclosed (REO) and auctions.
  • Applies Zillow's own filters server-side: price range, minimum beds/baths, home types, days on Zillow and sort order.
  • Pages through Zillow's result pages up to its own limit, with no browser and no cookies required.
  • Optionally opens each listing's detail page for the listing agent, photos, description, year built, HOA and authoritative distress flags.
  • Fields Zillow doesn't publish for a listing (no price, no Zestimate, no photos) are left out of that record rather than filled with a placeholder.

Use cases

  • Distressed-inventory investing — track foreclosure and pre-foreclosure properties before they hit the broader market.
  • Wholesaling — build a pre-foreclosure outreach list for a ZIP or neighborhood.
  • Auction prep — research properties scheduled for a foreclosure auction ahead of time.
  • REO acquisition — surface pre-market bank-owned properties for buy-and-hold portfolios.
  • Market intelligence — quantify distress rates by city, ZIP or county over time.

FAQ

What's the difference between the distress types? foreclosure is an active foreclosure listing for sale (Zillow's foreclosure filter also includes bank-owned homes); bankOwned marks a home the lender already owns (REO); preForeclosure is a pre-market record early in the foreclosure process, not yet listed; preMarketForeclosure is a pre-market record the bank has already taken back; auction is a property scheduled for a foreclosure auction.

How many results can I get per run? Up to 5,000 via maxItems. Zillow itself caps any single search at 20 result pages (about 820 listings). If a market hits that cap, split it into several locations — by home type, ZIP or neighborhood — and pass them together; the slices are disjoint, so their union covers the full inventory (verified: Los Angeles distressed inventory = 1,349 listings; its seven home-type slices sum to exactly 1,349).

Do I need proxies or cookies? No. The actor routes its requests automatically and needs no account, cookie or API key from you.

Why do some listings have no price or Zestimate? Zillow genuinely doesn't show a price or estimate for many pre-market distressed records. Those fields are omitted from the record instead of being filled with a placeholder.

Does this include auction dates, unpaid balances or lender details? Zillow's own pages don't carry them, but auction listings link out to the auctioneer (postingUrl). Turn on includeAuctionDetails and the actor also reads the linked auction.com page, adding the auction date and sale time, occupancy, starting bid, loan principal, lender, servicer and foreclosure attorney (each when the auctioneer publishes it). Roughly two thirds of sampled Zillow auctions link to auction.com; other auctioneers and unpaid balances are not covered.

Is this affiliated with Zillow? No. This actor is an independent tool that reads publicly visible Zillow search results and is not affiliated with or endorsed by Zillow.
