    US Gas Price Tracker by State & Metro is an Apify Actor published by Dataspryva. It accepts 11 input fields, and mode is required. Each result can carry up to 8 declared fields, including scope, state, metro, regular, midGrade and premium. It costs $0.99 per 1,000 results on Apify's Free plan and $0.49 on the Business plan.

    What it does

    Get daily US gas and diesel prices without visiting a fuel-price site yourself. This actor reads the public AAA Fuel Gauge data and returns it as structured records at whichever level you need: one national figure, all 50 states plus DC, or metro/county breakdowns within specific states.

    What this actor does

    • Three scopes: a single national average, state-level averages (50 states + DC), or metro/county averages within chosen states
    • Four fuel grades: regular, mid-grade, premium, and diesel, each only included when the source actually publishes it
    • Historical comparison: yesterday, week-ago, month-ago, and year-ago prices alongside today's, plus derived cent-value changes for a chosen primary fuel
    • Narrowing filters: by state, by fuel grade, by a price band on the primary fuel, and by metro-name substring
    • Empty fields are omitted — a record only includes the fuel grades and history buckets the source actually published for it

    Use cases

    • Fleet and logistics planning — compare diesel prices across states before routing
    • News and editorial research — pull a "cheapest gas by state" snapshot on demand
    • Consumer price widgets — feed a national or regional average into a dashboard
    • Trend monitoring — watch changeFromYearAgo to catch large year-over-year price swings
    • Economic analysis — fuel prices as an input to broader cost-of-living or inflation tracking

    The publisher's output notes

    Output per record

    • scope — national, state, or metro
    • country — always US, present only on national records
    • state, stateCode — state name and two-letter code, present on state and metro records
    • metro — metro/county area name, present only on metro records
    • regular, midGrade, premium, diesel — current price in USD/gallon for each grade the source publishes
    • history — an object with yesterday, weekAgo, monthAgo, and yearAgo prices per fuel grade, when includeHistory is enabled (state/metro modes) or whenever the source publishes it on the home page (national mode, where includeHistory isn't consulted)
    • changeFromYesterday, changeFromWeekAgo, changeFromMonthAgo, changeFromYearAgo — price change in cents for the chosen primaryFuelType, when includeChangeMetrics is enabled and the matching history value exists
    • recordType — always fuelPrices
    • scrapedAt — when the record was fetched

    Questions

    Where does this data come from?

    The public AAA Fuel Gauge pages, the same averages AAA publishes for national, state, and metro-level gas prices.

    Do I need a login, cookies, or proxy?

    No. The source pages are fully public and the actor reads them without authentication.

    Does this cover countries outside the US?

    No, this is US-only; the underlying source does not publish averages for other countries.

    Why isn't per-station pricing included?

    This actor reports state and metro averages rather than individual gas-station prices, since AAA's own data is published at that level.

    How current is the data?

    AAA refreshes its averages daily, and each run reads the page live, so results reflect whatever AAA has published at run time.

    Why might a record be missing a fuel grade?

    Not every state or metro area has a published price for every grade (mid-grade and premium especially). The actor omits a grade from a record rather than sending a null or zero value.

    Why is a change metric like changeFromMonthAgo sometimes absent?

    It's only computed when both the current price and the matching history value are available for that record's primary fuel.

    What does metroNameFilter match against?

    A case-insensitive substring match against the metro/county area name as published by AAA (e.g. Albany-Schenectady-Troy, Nassau-Suffolk).
