    Amazon Wishlist & Registry Scraper: export to CSV, Excel or JSON

    Scrape public/shareable Amazon wishlists, gift lists, baby registries, and wedding registries from URLs you supply. Get list metadata, item ASINs, prices, quantities needed/purchased, priority, and comments.

    This tutorial runs Amazon Wishlist & Registry Scraper through the Apify API and saves what it returns as a CSV file, an Excel workbook or JSON, keeping the 8 columns its publisher puts first: recordType, title, type, accessClassification, asin, price, requestedQuantity and purchasedQuantity.

    What people export it for

    • Charity and hospital donation drives — export a shared wishlist so volunteers see current needs
    • Classroom supply lists — track what teachers still need, including requested vs purchased counts
    • Wedding/baby registry gift coordination — see remaining needs and the registry's event date
    • Gift-list price monitoring — watch current prices and rating changes on a shared list
    • Archiving a list you own or administer — export it before editing or deleting it

    Fix common problems

    What does BLOCKED mean?

    Every retry, including the automatic residential fallback, hit an Amazon anti-bot/CAPTCHA page instead of the list. The list may exist; re-run later. It is never reported as NOT_FOUND.

    Why is my price in a different currency than the marketplace?

    Amazon renders cross-border prices in the visitor's local currency (for example, a US registry viewed from Pakistan can show PKR). The price.currency field reports the currency Amazon actually displayed; marketplace.currency reports the list's own marketplace currency.

    Why are some items missing?

    Classic wishlists hide items the owner marked as purchased (the public page only offers an "Unpurchased" filter); some registries hide purchased items the same way. Gift-fund/contribution entries and deleted items may have no product card at all. When a gift-fund card does render, it is returned with its displayed name and a stable entry ID, but with no fabricated ASIN, product URL, or price. Amazon's own counters can therefore be higher than extractedItemCount.
